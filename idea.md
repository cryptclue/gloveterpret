# the idea

## where it came from

the concept starts with a simple observation: assistive tech for deaf accessibility has consistently been built around hearing people's comfort, not deaf people's language.

live captions serve hearing people who occasionally can't hear. they serve deaf people whose first language happens to be english. they don't serve — and were never really designed to serve, people whose first language is asl or bsl, because the people building those tools treated sign language as a format, not a language.

glovterpreter starts from the opposite side, discarding that assumption with a singular goal in mind where sign language is the output medium, not a workaround.

## the name

glovterpreter. it's a glove that interprets. the name anticipates the physical form factor in the roadmap, a glove-shaped device that physically signs on behalf of the system. for the current software build, it describes what the 3d hand model is doing: not animating, not displaying, but interpreting.

## the 3d hand choice

the visual output being a procedurally animated low-poly hand is not a concession to technical limitations. it's a design statement.

a text gloss output would say: "here is a representation of sign language in a format you can read." a signing hand says: "here is sign language." the medium is part of the message. for a tool whose entire purpose is to argue that sign language is a language and not a notation system, the output should be the language itself, not a description of it.

low-poly over realistic for the same reason: the goal is correct interpretation, not simulation of a human interpreter. a geometric, abstract hand that signs correctly makes no false promises about what the system is doing. it signs. that's the product.

## the pause as feature

every other speech-to-sign or speech-to-caption tool treats latency as a problem to minimize. glovterpreter treats the pause as a linguistic necessity.

the pause: the moment between a speaker completing a thought and the hand beginning to sign, is where interpretation happens. it's not a bug or a delay. it's the system doing the work that makes the output a real interpretation rather than a transcription in a different format.

this is a useful reframe for the pitch: glovterpreter isn't slower than captioning. it's aiming at doing something categorically different, and the pause is evidence of that.
